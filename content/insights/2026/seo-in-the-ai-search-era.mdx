I launched this site into the strangest search landscape in twenty years. The
old contract — rank on page one, collect the clicks — is quietly dissolving.

## What actually changed

Three shifts define search in 2026:

**Zero-click is now the default, not the exception.** Industry studies put the
majority of Google searches ending without a single click to any website —
[SparkToro measured ~59%](https://scandiweb.com/blog/zero-click-google-search-and-generative-ai/)
back in 2024 and the trend has only accelerated since AI Overviews rolled out.
In Google's full AI Mode interface, some analyses report
[over 90% of sessions ending with no external click](https://navoto.com/blog/how-ai-is-changing-seo-in-2026/).

**The answer box ate the results page.** When an AI summary is present,
[Pew Research found users click traditional results roughly half as often](https://scandiweb.com/blog/zero-click-google-search-and-generative-ai/).
Ranking first and being read are no longer the same thing.

**A new discipline emerged: GEO.** Generative Engine Optimization —
[formalised in academic work out of Princeton and IIT](https://www.deepmarketing.it/en/blog/zero-click-search-google-ai-sme-2026)
— optimises content to be *cited inside* AI-generated answers rather than
listed below them. The practical techniques look familiar to anyone who has
written good documentation: answer the question in the first two sentences,
keep claims factual and verifiable, use structured data, write extractable
self-contained passages.

## Does traditional SEO die, then?

No — and this is the part most hot takes get wrong. AI Overviews
[draw heavily from pages that already rank well](https://scandiweb.com/blog/zero-click-google-search-and-generative-ai/),
so classic technical SEO is what makes you *eligible* for citation in the
first place. GEO sits on top of SEO, not in place of it.

## What this means for an engineer's personal site

For a site like this one, the goal was never ad-revenue traffic — it's being
*found and trusted* by a recruiter, a hiring manager, or an engineer with the
same bug I just fixed. That reframes the strategy:

- **Machine-readable by design.** Server-rendered static HTML, JSON-LD
  (`Person` on the home page, `Article` on every post), semantic headings,
  and a sitemap. If an AI system parses this site, nothing is hidden behind
  client-side rendering.
- **Answer-first writing.** Every post's `description` field answers "what
  will I learn here" in one sentence — it doubles as the meta description and
  the extractable summary.
- **Verifiable specifics over generic advice.** Posts here document real
  builds with real configuration. Factual density is exactly what
  answer engines select for.

The deepest change is psychological: impressions and citations are the new
currency, clicks are a bonus. Write for the human who eventually arrives —
but structure for the machine that decides whether they ever do.
